# Pedagogy

**Experience → Observe → Wonder → Explore → Understand → Create → Reflect**

The cycle can start with an everyday event, a familiar object, or a child’s question. Use it as a reference for choosing an invitation. Families can pause at any point, stay with one observation, or return another day.

| Stage | How to support it |
| --- | --- |
| Experience | Start with something the child can encounter through their senses. |
| Observe | Invite a description of what changed, stayed the same, or caught their attention. |
| Wonder | Keep the child’s words and let their question guide what happens next. |
| Explore | Ask what they already think, what they could try, and what to watch for. Offer a small trial with familiar materials. |
| Understand | Help the child describe what they found and connect it to something they actually observed. Leave room for uncertainty. |
| Create | Offer a sketch, a model, a story, or another way to express the discovery. |
| Reflect | Invite a quiet pause or a gentle question about what surprised them, how it felt, or what they wonder now. |

## Writing prompts

Start with an action the family can take. “Find a shadow near a window. Trace its edge, then look again later. What changed?” gives the child something to investigate. A prompt about an explanation can come later, once the child has evidence to work with.

Offer younger children one concrete invitation at a time and let an adult record their words. Children ready for more can make a prediction, compare two observations, or choose what to vary. Treat these as options guided by the child’s interest and readiness.

If AI is introduced, use it to suggest a next observation, a question, or a simple trial. Allow space for the child to answer first. Offer explanations when the family is ready and connect them to what the family observed.

## Choosing a thinking routine

Start with the child's own words. Match the most recent relevant response before falling back to the original question or noticing. The browser uses inspectable word patterns for changed thinking, multiple viewpoints, physical change, and questions about what happens. A close look is the default when those patterns do not match. The app explains its suggestion and lets the parent choose another routine. Each recorded or skipped invitation makes room for a different one.

| Situation | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| A detail catches the child's attention | See–Think–Wonder invites a fresh look and a thought or question. |
| The child wonders how something behaves | Notice–Predict–Investigate starts with watching what happens. |
| A change can be watched or tried | Predict–Observe–Explain asks for a guess and looks for evidence before inviting an explanation. |
| Several people may see something differently | Circle of Viewpoints invites the child to consider another perspective. |
| New evidence changes an idea | I Used to Think… Now I Think… makes space to describe what changed and why. |

See–Think–Wonder, Circle of Viewpoints, and I Used to Think… Now I Think… were developed by Project Zero, a research center at the Harvard Graduate School of Education. Learning Compass writes its own parent-facing invitations. Notice–Predict–Investigate and Predict–Observe–Explain are inquiry approaches used here and are not attributed to Project Zero.
